01
Planning & Risk Assessment
Engagement setup · Materiality · Risk identification · SEFA determination
REQUIRED ALL TYPESNPO: Contribution risk focus
+
1.1
Engagement Letter & Acceptance
Before any audit work begins, HCLLP sends an engagement letter confirming scope, fees, responsibilities, and report types. For an NPO, this will specify: financial statement audit under GAAS, Yellow Book compliance (if applicable), and Single Audit under Uniform Guidance (if federal expenditures ≥ $750K).
// Example — HCLLP Context
Client: Albuquerque Community Food Bank. Prior year federal expenditures = $820,000. Engagement letter scope includes: (1) GAAS audit of financial statements, (2) Yellow Book compliance audit, (3) Single Audit of federal programs. Estimated fee: $28,000. Timeline: fieldwork August, report issued November.
1.2
Understand the Entity — Preliminary Review
The auditor reviews prior year financial statements, the IRS Form 990 (nonprofits file this annually — it's public), board meeting minutes, bylaws, and major grant agreements. The goal: understand how the NPO operates, who controls it, and where money comes from and goes.
Documents Reviewed
  • Prior year audited financials
  • IRS Form 990 (public document)
  • Board meeting minutes (all year)
  • Bylaws and conflict of interest policy
  • Major grant agreements
  • Organizational chart
NPO-Specific Questions
  • What % of revenue is government grants?
  • Are there donor-restricted endowments?
  • Has the board approved spending restrictions?
  • Any new programs started this year?
  • Any grants with unusual conditions?
  • Net asset deficit? Going concern risk?
1.3
Set Materiality — Planning & Performance
Materiality is the threshold below which a misstatement is too small to matter. The auditor calculates Planning Materiality (overall), Performance Materiality (lower, for individual tests), and Trivial/Clearly Inconsequential amount. For NPOs, the base is typically total expenses or total revenues.
// Materiality Calculation — NPO Example
Total Expenses = $1,840,000
Planning Materiality = 2% of total expenses = $36,800
Performance Materiality = 75% of planning = $27,600
Trivial amount = 5% of planning = $1,840

What this means: Any single error over $36,800 is material. The auditor designs tests to catch errors above $27,600. Errors below $1,840 are ignored entirely.
🤖
AuditAI — Calculation Engine: This is a prime Calculation Engine task. Given total expenses from the trial balance, auto-compute all three materiality figures using the applicable percentage. For NPOs the base is expenses; for governments it's revenues or expenditures; The base selection itself is a labeled input — the AI should know which base to use by client_type (NPO → total expenses; Govt → revenues or expenditures, whichever is larger).
1.4
Identify Significant Audit Areas & Inherent Risks
The auditor identifies which financial statement areas carry the highest risk of material misstatement. For NPOs, the top risks are always: contribution revenue recognition, functional expense allocation, and federal grant compliance. These become the focus areas that get the most audit work.
NPO Inherent Risk Areas
  • HIGH: Contribution revenue (restriction classification)
  • HIGH: Grant compliance (allowable costs)
  • HIGH: Functional expense allocation
  • MED: Related party transactions
  • MED: Going concern / liquidity
  • LOW: Investment valuation
Risk Assessment Workpaper Output
  • Risk of Material Misstatement matrix
  • Significant account list
  • Audit response for each risk
  • Overall engagement risk: Low/Med/High
  • Planning memo signed by engagement partner
1.5
SEFA Determination — Is Single Audit Required?
The auditor compiles (or reviews management's compilation of) the Schedule of Expenditures of Federal Awards. If total federal expenditures ≥ $750K, a Single Audit is required. The SEFA must list every federal award by CFDA/ALN number, federal agency, amount expended, and any pass-through amounts.
// NPO SEFA — Food Bank Example
CFDA/ALN | Federal Agency | Program | Expended
10.567 | USDA | TEFAP (Emergency Food) | $480,000
93.568 | HHS | Emergency Food & Shelter | $220,000
14.231 | HUD | ESG (Emergency Solutions) | $90,000
93.044 | HHS | Senior Nutrition Program | $75,000
TOTAL FEDERAL EXPENDITURES: $865,000 → SINGLE AUDIT REQUIRED
02
Internal Controls Assessment
Control environment · Key controls · Walkthroughs · Control deficiencies
REQUIRED ALL TYPESNPO: Board oversight, small staff risk
+
2.1
Understand the Control Environment
The control environment is the "tone at the top" — does management care about controls? For NPOs, the key questions are: Is the board actively engaged? Does it approve budgets, review financials monthly, and oversee management? Are there written policies? Most small NPOs have weak controls due to limited staff.
// Common NPO Control Weakness
Finding: A food bank has 3 accounting staff. One person receives cash donations, records them in the accounting system, AND deposits them at the bank. This is a lack of segregation of duties — the same person controls the entire cash process with no independent check. This is a Significant Deficiency (or Material Weakness if large amounts). The auditor must expand substantive testing to compensate for this control gap.
2.2
Walkthrough — Revenue Process
The auditor traces ONE transaction from beginning to end to verify the system works as described. For NPOs, the most important walkthrough is contribution/grant revenue: from receipt of cash/check → recording in GL → bank deposit → financial statement.
Walkthrough Steps
  • Receive donor check in mail
  • Finance director opens mail & logs check
  • Bookkeeper records in accounting system
  • Monthly reconciliation to bank statement
  • Board treasurer reviews monthly reports
Control Points Tested
  • Is the check log independent of recording?
  • Is revenue classified by restriction correctly?
  • Does someone independent reconcile to bank?
  • Does board review monthly financials?
  • Are unusual items escalated to leadership?
2.3
Grant Compliance Controls
For NPOs receiving federal grants, auditors test whether there are controls to ensure compliance with each grant's rules: Are costs allowable? Is procurement following federal standards? Are financial reports filed on time? Is there a subrecipient monitoring process?
🤖
AuditAI Context: Internal control workpapers for NPOs use consistent questionnaire structures. These are excellent training data for pattern recognition — the AI can learn that "IC questionnaire + NPO + contribution revenue" always leads to specific follow-up procedures. // Real Workpaper Example — Contribution Revenue Test
Auditor selects 30 contribution transactions above performance materiality ($27,600) from the revenue ledger. For each:

1. Obtain donor acknowledgment letter — verify amount, date, and any restriction language
2. Trace to bank deposit slip — verify cash was received
3. Verify GL coding — was it coded to "with restriction" or "without restriction" correctly?
4. If coded as "with restriction" — verify the restriction was still active at year-end
5. If a grant — verify the grant conditions were met before revenue was recognized

Finding example: $45,000 grant from HHS coded as revenue in March (when cash was received). But the grant agreement required the NPO to complete a training program first (a condition). Training wasn't completed until June. The $45,000 should have been a liability ("refundable advance") at March 31 year-end — material misstatement.
04
Compliance Testing (Single Audit)
Major program selection · Compliance requirements · Findings
Required if ≥$750K federalHigh regulatory risk
+
4.1
Determine Major Programs
Not all federal programs get tested equally. The auditor uses a risk-based approach (Step 1–6 in the Uniform Guidance) to identify "major programs" that require full compliance testing. For a small NPO with $865K total federal expenditures, typically 1–2 programs will be major.
// Major Program Determination — Food Bank
Type A threshold = $750K × 0.03 = $22,500
Type A programs (above threshold): TEFAP $480K ✓ — HHS Emergency Food $220K ✓
Type B programs: HUD ESG $90K, Senior Nutrition $75K

Step 2: Are Type A programs low-risk? TEFAP was audited last year with no findings → LOW RISK → still selected as major.
Result: TEFAP and HHS Emergency Food are Major Programs. Both receive full compliance testing. HUD ESG and Senior Nutrition are not tested unless risk-based selection adds them.
4.2
Test Compliance Requirements
The OMB Compliance Supplement lists 12 types of compliance requirements for each federal program. The auditor identifies which are "direct and material" to each major program and tests those. Common requirements: Activities Allowed, Eligibility, Procurement, Reporting, Subrecipient Monitoring.
Compliance RequirementWhat's TestedTest Procedure
Activities Allowed / UnallowedWere program funds spent only on allowable costs?Select 25 disbursements → verify each is an allowable cost per grant agreement
EligibilityDid program serve eligible recipients?Test 20 beneficiary files → verify income documentation, household size, eligibility determination
ProcurementWere purchases made following federal procurement standards?Test 15 purchases → verify competitive bidding, contract documentation, debarment checks
ReportingWere required federal reports filed on time and accurately?Obtain all required reports → agree amounts to accounting records → verify submission dates
Period of PerformanceWere costs incurred within the grant period?Test sample → verify dates on invoices fall within grant period
05
Reporting & Wrap-Up
Financial statement opinion · Yellow Book report · Single Audit reports · Management letter
Required — all types
+
Reports Issued — NPO with Single Audit
  • Independent Auditor's Report — Opinion on financial statements (unmodified, qualified, adverse, or disclaimer)
  • Report on Internal Control — Yellow Book report on significant deficiencies / material weaknesses
  • Report on Compliance — Yellow Book compliance with laws and regulations
  • Report on Federal Programs — Single Audit opinion on major programs
  • Schedule of Findings — Lists all findings with questioned costs
  • Management Letter — Non-material observations and recommendations
Finding Classification (if issues found)
  • Material Weakness: Severe IC deficiency — reasonable chance of material misstatement going undetected. Must be reported publicly.
  • Significant Deficiency: Less severe but still important IC weakness. Reported to those charged with governance.
  • Material Noncompliance: Compliance failure that is material to a federal program. Results in questioned costs.
  • Questioned Costs: Specific dollar amount of potentially improper expenditures.

CPA Firm Context (HCLLP): Local government clients include NM cities, counties, school districts, water districts, and transit authorities. Planning & Risk Assessment
Fund structure · GASB risk areas · Materiality (by fund) · SEFA
REQUIREDGOV: Fund-level materiality
+
1.1
Map the Fund Structure
Unlike NPOs with a single set of books, local governments have multiple funds — each is essentially a separate entity with its own budget and financial statements. The first planning step is mapping every fund and understanding what each one does.
// Example: County of Rio Arriba, NM — Fund Map
Fund | Type | Budget | Key Risk
General Fund | Governmental | $12.4M | Payroll, vendor payments
Road Fund | Special Revenue| $3.1M | Capital outlay, FHWA grants
Debt Service Fund | Governmental | $1.8M | Bond payments
Capital Projects Fund | Governmental | $5.2M | Construction contracts
Water Enterprise Fund | Proprietary | $2.4M | Revenue bonds, rate setting
Risk Management Fund | Internal Svc | $0.8M | Self-insurance reserves
Pension Trust Fund | Fiduciary | $— | Investment returns, distributions
1.2
Set Materiality — Government-Wide AND Per Major Fund
Local governments need TWO levels of materiality: one for the government-wide financial statements and one for each "major fund." A misstatement in the Water Fund might be immaterial to the whole government but material to just that fund. Both must be set.
// Gov-Wide Materiality Example
Total Revenues (Govt-wide) = $18.6M
Planning Materiality = 1.5% of revenues = $279,000
Performance Materiality = 75% = $209,250

General Fund materiality (separate) = 2% of $12.4M = $248,000
Water Fund materiality (separate) = 3% of $2.4M = $72,000
1.3
Identify Government-Specific Risk Areas
Local governments have distinct risk areas that don't exist for NPOs. The auditor must flag all of these during planning because each requires specialized workpapers and often specialist involvement (actuary, appraiser).
GOV-ONLY Risk Areas
  • GASB 68 Pension — HIGH: Net Pension Liability requires actuary. Often largest balance sheet item.
  • GASB 75 OPEB — HIGH: Retiree health benefits. Similar complexity to pensions.
  • Capital Assets — HIGH: Roads, bridges, infrastructure — huge values, poor tracking common.
  • Long-Term Debt — HIGH: Bond covenants must be tested. GASB 87 leases added new complexity.
  • Fund Balance Classification — MED: GASB 54 five-way classification.
Standard (Same as NPO) Risk Areas
  • Cash and bank reconciliations
  • Accounts payable cutoff
  • Payroll and benefits
  • Federal grant compliance
  • Related party transactions
  • Going concern / financial condition
02
Internal Controls — Governmental
Elected officials · Weak segregation of duties · IT general controls · Budget compliance
GOV unique: Budget controls
+
2.1
Budget Controls — Unique to Governments
Local governments are legally required to stay within their adopted budgets. Overspending a budget is NOT just bad management — it can be illegal. Auditors test whether actual expenditures exceeded appropriations at the fund/department level (budgetary compliance). This control test has NO equivalent in NPO audits.
// Budgetary Compliance Test
General Fund Legal Appropriation (Police Dept): $3,200,000
Actual Expenditures: $3,340,000
OVER budget by $140,000 — FINDING: Expenditures exceeded legal appropriation.

This is a compliance finding under state budget law, not just a management observation. It will appear in the Yellow Book compliance report.
2.2
Segregation of Duties — Small Government Challenge
Many small NM cities and counties have 2–3 finance staff. One finance director + one clerk = cannot have proper segregation. The auditor documents this and adjusts testing — more substantive work is required when controls can't be relied upon.

// GASB 68 Pension Workpaper — Step by Step
Step 1: Obtain the NM PERA actuarial valuation report (state sends this to all participating employers)
Step 2: Find the county's Employer Allocation % in the report (e.g., Rio Arriba County = 0.83% of total PERA liability)
Step 3: Apply 0.83% to total PERA Net Pension Liability ($2.7B) = County NPL = $22.4M
Step 4: Agree this to the county's balance sheet — does it match?
Step 5: Verify pension expense for the year: service cost + interest − expected returns + amortization of deferred items
Step 6: Trace deferred outflows (employer contributions after measurement date) to payment records
Step 7: Trace deferred inflows (experience gains/losses) to actuarial report
Step 8: Recalculate the amortization of each deferred component

This workpaper is 30–50 pages for a mid-size government. The Calculation Engine should auto-verify all arithmetic in steps 3, 5, and 8.

Compliance Testing (Single Audit)
Federal programs · State compliance · Budget law · Debt covenants
Required if ≥$750K federalGOV: State law compliance layer added
+
⚡
Key difference from NPO: Local governments have an additional compliance layer — state laws and regulations. In New Mexico, HCLLP must also test compliance with the NM Audit Act, the NM Procurement Code, and NM Budget Act. NPOs don't have this state compliance layer. This generates additional workpapers that are GOV-only.
Federal Compliance (same as NPO)
  • Major program determination (SEFA)
  • Compliance supplement requirements
  • Activities allowed, eligibility, procurement
  • Reporting, subrecipient monitoring
  • Schedule of Findings & Questioned Costs
State Compliance (GOV-ONLY)
  • NM Budget Act — expenditures vs. appropriations
  • NM Procurement Code — competitive bidding
  • NM Audit Act — timely filing of audit report
  • Debt covenants — revenue bonds, NMFA loans
  • Investment Policy compliance

NM-Specific Requirement
  • All NM government audits must be filed with the NM Office of State Auditor (OSA)
  • Filed within 6 months of fiscal year-end (June 30 FYE → December 31 deadline)
  • OSA reviews for completeness and may issue comments
  • HCLLP coordinates with OSA on first-time findings
  • This state-level oversight has no NPO equivalent
